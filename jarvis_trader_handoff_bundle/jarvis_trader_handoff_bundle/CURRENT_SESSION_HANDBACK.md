## 6) Key decisions made — architectural choices, parameter values, tradeoffs

Because no codebase was accessible, these were **strategic / workflow decisions**, not software-architecture decisions.

### Major strategic decisions made in-session

1. **Primary objective prioritized as reinstatement**
   - User explicitly said reinstatement is the top priority.
   - Bonus recovery treated as secondary.

2. **Communication strategy set to `email first, then short text`**
   - Rationale: create a written record while keeping tone professional.
   - Tradeoff: slower than a call, but much cleaner evidentiary posture.

3. **Do not lead with legal threats**
   - Rationale: maximize chance of reopening dialogue and reinstatement.
   - Tradeoff: slightly softer leverage in the very first message.

4. **Do not mention AA / sobriety-path language**
   - Rationale: avoid language that could imply underlying addiction or partial admission.
   - Tradeoff: slightly fewer "good faith" optics, but much cleaner factual posture.

5. **Use objective-testing framing**
   - Preferred language:
     - willing to provide chemical dependency evaluation
     - willing to provide available negative test results
     - willing to submit to immediate testing / fitness-for-duty evaluation
   - Rationale: shows cooperation without admitting the accusation.

6. **Do not volunteer that treatment was not completed in the initial status email**
   - Rationale: avoid shifting the frame from status clarification to non-compliance.
   - Tradeoff: issue may still arise later and then must be answered directly.

7. **Sequence legal asks**
   - First: status clarification / reinstatement request.
   - Later: termination-reason request if termination is confirmed.
   - Tradeoff: preserves reinstatement posture before pivoting to formal legal process.

8. **Use DATWA / MHRA as core legal analysis**
   - DATWA became especially important after user clarified that no separate written testing policy existed.
   - MHRA "regarded as" disability theory treated as plausible but more fact-intensive.

### Parameter-like decisions / wording choices

- Preferred tone for first contact: **balanced / professional / not emotional**.
