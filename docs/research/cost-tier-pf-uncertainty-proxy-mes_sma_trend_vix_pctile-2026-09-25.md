## 3. What is NOT yet a formal confidence interval

The critique explicitly states (`expectancy.notes` and `red_flags[4]`):

> "Confidence intervals on PF are not directly reported as bootstrap CI in the snapshot — the cost-tier spread (1.508–1.706) is a proxy but not a formal CI."

This record inherits that honesty. The cost-tier spread is:

- **A sensitivity bound, not a sampling distribution.** It is generated by changing the cost model, not by resampling trades. It answers "how does PF move if costs are higher/lower?" not "what is the sampling uncertainty on PF given the 122 observed trades?"
- **One-dimensional.** It varies only slippage/commission, not trade outcomes, order, or parameter instability.
- **Not a bootstrap CI.** A bootstrap CI on PF would resample the 122 trade P&L series (with or without walk-forward window structure) and report, e.g., the 2.5th/97.5th percentile of the resulting PF distribution. That figure is not in the verdict snapshot.

The formal CI gap is recorded in `docs/research/backtest-critique-mes_sma_trend_vix_pctile-2026-09-24.json` open_items[0]; this record documents the proxy-acceptance leg pending sponsor sign-off per section 6.

## 4. Why the proxy is acceptable at this stage

Three reasons, all from the critique's own evidence:

1. **Magnitude.** The spread's lower bound (1.508) is still 50% above breakeven. Profit factor is not near the 1.0–1.2 warning band under any modeled cost tier, so the dominant risk the uncertainty quantification would flag — "maybe the edge disappears under realistic costs" — is already ruled out by the four-tier stress test.

2. **Drawdown uncertainty is already quantified separately.** The critique reports `mc_95th_dd = 0.89%` from a Monte Carlo drawdown analysis. The critique notes the DSR (0.9688 grid) is **not** a drawdown-survival estimate; the Monte Carlo figure is the actual drawdown-survival evidence. That leaves profit factor as the one remaining metric without its own uncertainty interval — a gap, but not a blind spot across the whole scorecard.

3. **Sample size is limited but adequate for the proxy's purpose.** 122 trades over 7.3 years is sparse for a tight bootstrap CI; a bootstrap run would be wide and would not change the promotion decision at this score (73/100, REFINE band). The critique already flags "below the ≥200-trade ideal" in the sample-size dimension.
